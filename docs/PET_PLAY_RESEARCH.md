# Pet-play research notes for Mara v0.1.12

## Scope and limitations

The open web contains far more material than can be exhaustively catalogued. This is a curated set of academic, community, and consent/safety references inspected for this release, not a claim to list every internet source. These references describe real-world adult roleplay. The Mara mod translates selected non-explicit ideas into fictional game events. Real animals are not participants in pet play.

## Common play patterns

Community guides describe pet play as a broad roleplay style rather than one fixed script. Common elements include handler/pet roles, animal-inspired movement, simple commands, following or heel, sit/stay/come/down/place, paw tricks, fetch, grooming, toys, praise, affection, quiet waiting, and a distinct roleplay headspace. Some scenes are playful or soothing; others may include power exchange. The specific mix depends on prior agreement.

## Negotiation, consent, and stopping

The National Coalition for Sexual Freedom's guidance says activities should be explicitly agreed to before they begin, participants should understand risks and boundaries, consent can be withdrawn, and an agreed word or signal should stop the activity. Its negotiation guide also covers roles, activities, gear, health needs, safety plans, and aftercare. The UK Kink Guide emphasises that a handler label or a quiet/non-verbal role does not remove the adult participant's agency; an accessible out-of-role stop cue and exit remain important.

## Physical and emotional care

The practical guides consistently recommend soft surfaces and limiting time on hands and knees; wrists and knees can become strained. They warn that collars should not restrict breathing and that leads must not be used to pull on the neck. They also advise planning a way to stop if normal speech is limited, monitoring for fatigue or distress, and allowing a gradual transition out of role with water, comfort, and conversation. Food play should be hygienic; the game therefore keeps the pet bowl meal as ordinary prepared food, not animal food.

## Academic research

- Wignall, L. & McCormack, M. (2017), “An Exploratory Study of a New Kink Activity: 'Pup Play',” *Archives of Sexual Behavior*, 46(3), 801-811. The study used interviews with 30 gay and bisexual men and discusses roleplay, relaxation, and headspace. Its sample is limited and should not be treated as representative of every pet-play participant. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26666883/
- Wignall, L., McCormack, M., Cook, T. & Jaspal, R. (2022), “Findings From a Community Survey of Individuals Who Engage in Pup Play,” *Archives of Sexual Behavior*, 51(7), 3637-3646. This community survey included 733 participants, primarily from the US, and examined how participants engage in pup play and its social and sexual elements. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9556369/
- “The Psychology of Puppy Play: A Phenomenological Investigation” (2019) reports themes including sexual pleasure, relaxation, adult play, self-expression, and community. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6746682/

## How v0.1.12 uses the research

The random training and cushion-order pools use common low-risk roleplay examples: commands, follow/heel, paw, fetch, place, grooming, and settling. The new event text keeps crawling brief and on soft surfaces, avoids neck pulling, includes stop choices, and adds water and a comfort check after training. These are design choices for a fictional game, not a real-world instruction manual or a claim that every pet-play dynamic uses the same activities.
